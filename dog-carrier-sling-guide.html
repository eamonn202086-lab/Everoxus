Small dog owners know the problem — your dog wants to be with you everywhere, but traditional carriers are bulky, awkward, and leave you with one hand permanently occupied. A dog sling solves this entirely. Your dog sits securely against your body, your hands stay free, and your dog gets the closeness and security they crave. It's one of those products that small dog owners discover and immediately wonder how they managed without it.

Who Is a Dog Sling For?

A dog carrier sling works best for:

  • Small breeds — generally suited to dogs under 6-7kg. Chihuahuas, Pomeranians, Shih Tzus, Maltese, French Bulldogs, and similar breeds
  • Older or injured dogs — dogs that can't walk long distances but still need to be out and about
  • Puppies — socialisation walks where the puppy needs to see the world but isn't fully vaccinated or gets tired quickly
  • Anxious dogs — being held close to their owner's body is naturally calming for anxious dogs

Sling vs Traditional Carrier — What's the Difference?

A traditional carrier is a rigid or semi-rigid bag with handles or a shoulder strap. It keeps your dog fully enclosed and separated from your body. A sling wraps across your body like a crossbody bag, with your dog sitting in an open pouch against your chest or hip. The key differences:

  • Hands-free — slings leave both hands completely free, traditional carriers occupy one hand
  • Closeness — slings keep your dog against your body, which is more comforting for most dogs
  • Visibility — your dog can see out and be seen, which is better for socialisation
  • Weight distribution — a well-designed sling distributes weight across your shoulder and back, making it more comfortable for longer outings

How to introduce your dog to a sling

Some dogs take to a sling immediately. Others need a gradual introduction. Here's how to make it smooth:

  • Day 1-2 — leave the sling on the floor and let your dog sniff and investigate it at their own pace
  • Day 3-4 — put treats inside the sling while it's on the floor. Let your dog climb in and out voluntarily
  • Day 5 — hold the sling in your arms and place your dog inside while standing still. Reward generously
  • Day 6+ — put the sling on and place your dog inside. Start with short sessions indoors before taking it outside

Never force a dog into a sling. A positive, gradual introduction means your dog will eventually climb in voluntarily.

Safety tips

  • Always keep one hand on your dog when navigating stairs, crowds, or uneven terrain
  • Check that your dog's head stays above the sling edge at all times for proper airflow
  • Don't use a sling as a substitute for a proper carrier on public transport or in vehicles
  • Monitor your dog for signs of overheating in warm weather — slings reduce airflow compared to walking
