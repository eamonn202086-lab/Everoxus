How to Keep Your Dog Safe in the Car — A Simple Guide for Dog Owners

By Everoxus · Updated May 2026 · #ad affiliate links

Most dog owners drive with their dog unrestrained in the back seat without thinking twice about it. But an unrestrained dog in a moving vehicle is a genuine safety risk — for the dog, for you, and for other passengers. In an emergency stop at 40mph, a 22lb dog becomes a 660lb projectile. It's one of those risks that feels unlikely until it isn't.

The fix is simple, inexpensive, and takes about 30 seconds to set up.

Is It Illegal to Drive with an Unrestrained Dog?

In many countries, driving with an unrestrained animal in the vehicle can result in a fine under distracted driving or animal welfare legislation. Beyond the legal side, unrestrained dogs can interfere with the driver, jump between seats, and cause serious accidents. Restraining your dog is the responsible choice regardless of legality.

What Are Your Options?

There are three main ways to restrain a dog in a car:

  • Dog seat belt / harness — clips into your existing seatbelt buckle. Simple, affordable, works in any car.
  • Dog crate or carrier — secured in the boot or back seat. Better for anxious dogs or very small breeds.
  • Dog car seat — elevated booster seat with a tether. Best for small dogs who like to look out the window.

For most dogs and most situations, a quality seat belt harness is the simplest and most practical solution.

Important — use with a harness, not a collar

Never attach a car restraint to a collar. In a sudden stop the force on a collar can cause serious neck injury. Always use a chest harness as the attachment point. If your dog doesn't have a harness yet, pick one up before using a car seat belt restraint.
